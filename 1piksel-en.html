01 — Brief

Where it
came from

Studio 1 Piksel is a young, ambitious advertising agency. Their work is built on four pillars — websites, graphic design, advertising print, and business photography.

The task was to create a cohesive, printed service catalogue that would clearly present all offerings to potential clients. The document had to look professional while also standing out from the competition.

The key challenge: presenting four different service categories — each with their own process — in a single cohesive document, so clients would know exactly what to expect at every stage.

A minimalist black-and-white base with a deep red accent. The red appears in the gradients of the process cards — energetic but not aggressive. Against white, it creates a premium effect.

Typography

Futura Bold + Regular + Book — headings and numbering

The contrast between bold black numbering (01, 02, 03...) and the delicate body text creates rhythm. Readability over decoration — a B2B catalogue must be functional.

Process diagram — a consistent system element

Each of the four services has its own process diagram. Cards with a black-to-red gradient and white icons form a cohesive, recognizable system element. When a client sees the diagram, they think: "I know exactly what I'm signing up for."

05 — Takeaways

What a well-designed
catalogue delivers

01

Hierarchy over decoration

A B2B catalogue is a sales tool, not a portfolio. Typographic hierarchy — large section numbers, bold keywords, process diagrams — guides the client through the document before they have a chance to put it down.

02

Repetition builds a system

The same process layout appears in every section. Consistency is professionalism. The client understands the structure after the first page and never has to re-learn it.

03

White space is an active element

White paper isn't background — it's breathing room. Wide margins and deliberate empty areas make the document feel more expensive and considered. Designed emptiness equals trust.
